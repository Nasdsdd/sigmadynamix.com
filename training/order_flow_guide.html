Reading the order flow

Four tools sit in the FLOW bar at the top right of the training charts. They show where the volume traded and who was aggressive.

Key level=Authorization. It is the only thing that allows you to trade.
Order flow=Context. Big orders, POC, VAH, VAL and CVD tell you what is happening in the market. They are not entry points.

Once a level has given you an authorization, use these tools to watch how price reacts: on the level itself, or on a return to a POC, a VAH, a VAL or a zone of big orders. If price bounces, you can enter on the bounce. If it breaks, you can enter on the break. The decision is always yours.

Two words to know

Aggressive buyer: someone who buys at market and lifts the offer. Aggressive seller: someone who sells at market and hits the bid. Every contract traded has one of the two. The order flow tools count them.

Everything is revealed at replay time: a minute enters the profile and the delta as it trades, nothing from the future is shown.

VP Volume profile of the session

A horizontal histogram on the right of the chart: how many contracts traded at each price since 09:30 New York. It builds up during the session. It is the most useful tool of the day: its shape tells you where price will slow down and where it will accelerate.

Peaks of volume = a fight

A wide part of the profile is a price where buyers and sellers fought hard. When price comes back there, it usually slows down and hesitates: this is where to watch who wins.

Gaps in the profile = acceleration

A thin part of the profile is a price where little traded: nobody defended it. When price enters a gap, it usually accelerates until it reaches the next peak.

What you see

  • green part of a bar: aggressive buying at that price; red part: aggressive selling
  • POC (point of control): the price with the most volume, the market's "fair price" of the day
  • VAH / VAL: top and bottom of the value area, the zone holding 70 % of the volume

How to read it

  • Price inside the value area: the market is balanced, it tends to rotate back to the POC
  • Price leaving the value area and holding outside: the market is looking for a new value, moves can extend
  • Between two peaks, the gap is the path: price tends to travel fast from one peak to the next

PREV Previous session

The profile of the previous session (09:30–16:00 NY), drawn in pink on the left, with its three levels as pink dashed lines: pPOC, pVAH, pVAL. When one of them is off screen, a small tag at the right edge shows its price and an arrow (▲ above, ▼ below).

  • Opening inside yesterday's value area: expect rotation between pVAL and pVAH
  • Opening outside: watch the first return to pVAH or pVAL. Accepted back inside → the move often travels to the pPOC. Rejected → the move away usually continues
  • pPOC, pVAH and pVAL are frequently tested: treat them like any other key level

CVD Delta and cumulative delta

A band at the bottom of the chart. Delta (bars) = aggressive buying minus aggressive selling in each candle: green above zero, red below. CVD (blue line) = the running total of the delta since 09:30 NY. The two dotted lines are the highest and lowest CVD of the opening range (09:30–09:45).

Confirmation

Price and CVD rise together: buyers are pushing the move. Price and CVD fall together: sellers are. The move is healthy.

Divergence

Price makes a new high but the CVD does not (or a new low without the CVD): fewer aggressive traders behind the move. Not a signal by itself, but a warning that the move is tiring, especially at a key level.

BIG Big orders

A bubble on the candle at the price where an unusually large aggressive order traded. The number is its size in contracts. The menu next to BIG sets the minimum size shown (≥20, ≥30, ≥50, ≥100): raise it to keep only the largest.

What a big order means: a lot of volume traded at that place. Nothing more. It tells you a price is important to the market right now, not which way price will go.

It can reinforce a break

Big orders on a level that gives way: the volume pushed through and the move can extend. A break of a level backed by big orders is a strong break.

It can mark a reversal

Big buyers do not mean price will explode upwards. Their orders can be absorbed by sellers waiting at that price, and the market turns. The same for big sellers at a low.

aggressive buyer aggressive seller filled = price moved its way in the next 30 s hollow = absorbed, price did not move its way pale = still under watch (30 s)

So do not read a bubble as "buy" or "sell". Read it as "a lot happened here", then watch how price reacts when it comes back to that zone: does it hold and bounce, or does it go through?

Note. For past days, Rithmic does not say which side each trade was on: the side of a big order is estimated from the price move, within the exact buy and sell totals of each price and minute. Read single bubbles with care; several bubbles at the same price are more meaningful.

Putting it together

The order of things never changes:

  1. The key level gives the authorization. No authorization, no trade, whatever the order flow shows.
  2. The order flow gives the context. Where is the POC, the VAH, the VAL, yesterday's levels? Were there big orders near the level? Is the CVD following the price?
  3. Price gives the answer. Watch the reaction on the level, or on a return to a POC, a VAH, a VAL or a big-order zone. It bounces: you can enter on the bounce. It breaks: you can enter on the break.
  4. You decide. Direction, entry, stop and take profits are your call. These tools help you read the market; they never take the decision for you.
